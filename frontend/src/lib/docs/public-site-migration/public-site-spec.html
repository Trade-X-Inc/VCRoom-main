01How to use this document

Two readers. The founder reviews positioning and copy. CC reads §03–§07 as law, then builds each page in §A–§H exactly as specified.

Honesty rule

A waitlist page never implies a feature exists. It describes what the workflow will do and offers early access. No screenshots of unbuilt screens. No invented customer logos, counts, or testimonials anywhere on the site. One fabricated signal contaminates the whole record positioning.

02Positioning the site must carry

The site exists to do three jobs at once. Every page serves at least one.

JobHow the site does it
Enter the categoryRank for what the buyer actually searches — virtual data room, deal management, due diligence — then reframe to the record. The doorway is not the destination.
Establish institutionSurface area, published pricing, an open standard, security documentation, and a legal footer signal a platform, not a startup. Breadth is credibility.
Convert the deploying sideAudience pages address the buyer with budget — leads, advisors, family offices, funds — in their own vocabulary, benefit first.
The one line

A deal room that runs the transaction, not just the documents. Everything two parties do between deciding to proceed and closing, kept as a record that holds.

What the site is not. Not a marketplace. Not a directory. It never claims to verify a party, score a deal, or introduce anyone. It records who asserted what, when, under what warranty. Parties judge.

07Voice & copy rules

The register is institutional documentation. Experts say the most in the fewest words. Every sentence earns its place.

Hold to

  • Sentences under twenty words.
  • Active voice. Named actors.
  • Specific over adjectival. A number over "powerful".
  • Terms of art used correctly, or not at all.
  • Benefit first for the buyer, mechanism second.
  • One idea per paragraph.

Never

  • Exclamation marks.
  • Verified · trusted · genuine · seamless · revolutionary.
  • AI-powered · smart · intelligent as adjectives.
  • Invented scores, percentages, counts.
  • Claims of a licensed activity we do not perform.
  • Startup exuberance. This buyer distrusts it.
SayNever say
A record that holds when it is examined.Bank-grade security you can trust.
Raise every diligence request in one notice.Streamlined, frictionless diligence.
Published price. No procurement.Flexible enterprise pricing.
The pack survives between raises.Reusable, always-on data.

How it works/how-it-works
NAV · PlatformSTATUS · Live-ready

The seven-state lifecycle, in order, each state a mechanism with a benefit. This is where a serious buyer decides we are real.

The lifecycle

Seven states, one record.

Every raise runs the same spine. Brief to close. Each step recorded, referenced, and defensible after the fact.

The seven states (numbered) 01 Brief. A thin, always-current one-pager. Link-shared, tracked, revocable.
02 Present. A controlled render to one named counterparty. Expiry and access log.
03 Confidentiality. An individually signed, recorded NDA before any confidential view.
04 Diligence. Requests raised in one batch per stage. A new batch waits for the current one to close.
05 Terms. Propose, accept, counter, per term. Append-only. Locks on mutual acceptance.
06 Conditions. Nothing closes until every condition is satisfied or waived on the record.
07 Close. A sealed export — chain, terminal hash, parties, references — issued to both sides.
Section — The single-notice rule (callout)Borrowed from documentary credit: an examining party raises every discrepancy in one notice. It ends the most-cited founder complaint in fundraising. It costs nothing — it is a constraint, not a feature.
Section — What we never doWe do not match, rank, recommend, or introduce. We do not assert a party is genuine. We record who asserted what, when, under what warranty. Parties judge.

title: How it works — the seven-state closing lifecycle
description: Brief, present, NDA, diligence, terms, conditions, close. One recorded spine for every private-capital raise.

The record/the-record
NAV · Platform menu · footerSTATUS · Live-ready

The moat page. Why the record is the product, why it persists between raises, and why that makes the next round trivial. This is the answer to "another data room?".

The durable asset

The record is the product.

A data room ends at download. Ours ends at a sealed record that survives the raise, the audit, and the round after this one.

Section — What the record isAppend-only and hash-chained. Every entry carries its predecessor's hash, so alteration is detectable. Every citable object has a reference number with a check digit. A mistyped reference is caught without a lookup.
Section — It survives the raiseThe disclosure pack is versioned and never deleted. It carries forward. The next round starts from a clean, referenced history instead of a fresh spreadsheet. Cap-table cleanliness is a frequent cause of later-round failure. We keep it clean by default.
Section — Tamper-evident, disclosedWhere a periodic root is published for third-party verification, we describe it plainly as tamper-evident timestamping. A verification method that cannot be published cannot be relied on.
Section — The sealed export (reference strip)Show the artifact: parties, references, terminal hash, timestamps, signature block. "This is what you file. This is what holds."

title: The record — the durable asset behind every closed deal
description: Hash-chained, referenced, sealed. A closing record that survives the raise and makes the next round trivial.

Reference

How to read a reference number.

Every citable object carries one. It tells you the organisation, the type, the year and the sequence — and it self-checks.

BodyFormat: {ORG}-{TYP}-{YYYY}-{SEQ}-{CD}. Worked example: ATLS01-RSE-2026-000042-31. The check digit uses the IBAN algorithm, so a mistype is caught without a lookup. Sequences are gapless; a gap is an incident, not a correction.

description: The reference scheme, check digit, and gapless sequencing behind every citable object in the record.
